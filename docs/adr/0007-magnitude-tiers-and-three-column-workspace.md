# Magnitude tiers and the three-column workspace

Shifts needed a notion of relative importance, but a fully hand-sorted list would demand constant curation. Instead, each Redshift and Blueshift carries a **Magnitude** — First through Fourth, with First the highest — and within a Magnitude shifts are deliberately unordered: everything in a tier is roughly equivalent and the choice of what to do next is played by ear. The name comes from astronomy's magnitude scale, where lower numbers mean brighter stars, so "First Magnitude" naturally reads as top priority even though it inverts the usual p0-style numbering. Magnitudes rank bodies of work; they stay orthogonal to North Stars, which designate individual Blueshift Stars as today's focus — the glossary's rule that "priority" is not a synonym for North Star is preserved by keeping the word out of the vocabulary entirely.

The workspace renders Redshifts on the left, the selected shift's Stars in the middle, and Blueshifts on the right, so the top Magnitudes of both kinds sit in view at once and Stars of both kinds can be balanced against each other. Alternatives considered: star-level priorities (finer-grained, but heavier and deferred until tiers prove insufficient), flexible tier counts (more tiers collapse back into custom sorting), and absorbing North Stars into the top tier (they answer different questions and North Star machinery is already established). New shifts start at Fourth Magnitude — promotion must be explicit.
